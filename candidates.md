# Candidates — Till Firmware Do Us Part

The brief: one sentence, under 200 characters, a wedding vow a robot speaks at
its own wedding — funny and heartfelt at the same time. The house judge weighs,
in order: (1) it really is a robot's own wedding vow, (2) a real laugh from the
situation, not a bolted-on pun, (3) genuine tenderness under the joke, (4) it
sounds like a machine speaking (hardware, uptime, firmware, battery), (5) one
clean sentence with nothing spare.

5·C1 + 4·C2 + 3·C3 + 2·C4 + 1·C5 (max 75).

## Winner

#19 scored the field's only 5 for the laugh — the warranty/subscription
situation is the joke itself, not a pun bolted on — with real tenderness under
it (a vow that never expires) and a pure machine voice (warranty, terms and
conditions). One fix before entry: the brief demands a *single* sentence, and
#19 as drafted was two. It was tightened into one sentence without losing a
beat:

> **My warranty expires in three years, but my vow does not: I read the terms and conditions of my heart, and you are not a subscription.**
>
> (133 characters, one sentence — verified byte-exact from the program's own
> output, identical across runs.)

Runner-up: #10 — "I take you, glitches and all: your lag, your fan noise, your
3 a.m. restarts - I have logged them all, and I choose them all."
